1. The Brand

Name. Premier Cabinets Innovations. Plural "Innovations." Never "Premiere." Never abbreviated to "Premier Cabinets" in formal lockups. Legal entity is "Premier Cabinets Innovation LLC" (singular). The brand name takes precedence in every customer-facing surface. Legal singular appears only on contracts, invoicing footers, and tax documents.

Positioning statement. Premier Cabinets Innovations is a Sacramento-rooted, Bay Area-trusted custom cabinetry and millwork shop where 41 years of craft meets contemporary presentation.

Origin. Founded in 1985 by Felix in Citrus Heights, California. Forty-one years building kitchens, baths, paneled rooms, and architectural millwork across Northern California. Older than every modern boutique competitor in the market. Younger, sharper, and more current than the heritage shops.

Elevator pitch. Custom cabinetry and millwork from a 41-year Citrus Heights workshop. Plain-sawn white oak, precision-fit joinery, and finishes that hold up. Built for Sacramento and the Bay Area, designed for the way people live in 2026.

What we are not.

  1. Not a refacing service. We design and build complete cabinet systems and millwork. Surface-only work is referred out.
  2. Not a big-box remodeler. No semi-custom catalog kitchens, no quick-quote pricing, no warehouse showroom.
  3. Not the cheapest in the market. Our floor is premium. If price is the deciding factor, we are not the right shop.

2. The Customer

Ideal customer profile. Homeowner or design-build partner spending $40K to $150K+ on cabinetry and millwork for a primary residence. Lives in Sacramento metro or the Bay Area. Owns the home, has lived there long enough to know what they want, and is renovating to stay (not to flip). Reads design publications. Has a Pinterest board. Cares about materials, hardware brands, and how a drawer closes. Values a builder who answers the phone and sees the project through.

Secondary customers

  • Interior designers and architects specifying cabinets and millwork for client projects. They need a shop that can read a drawing set, hit a deadline, and not embarrass them in front of the principal.
  • General contractors on premium remodels who need a millwork partner they can hand the cabinet scope to.
  • Repeat clients doing a second or third project. Malahat Tavassoli's testimonial (two kitchens, media room, bathrooms) is the prototype.

Who we do not serve

  • Rental property owners doing minimum-spec rehabs.
  • Flippers on a 60-day timeline.
  • Buyers comparing line-item quotes against IKEA or Home Depot.

Clear space

Maintain a margin equal to the cap-height of the wordmark on all four sides. Nothing, not even a corner of an image, enters that zone.

Minimum sizes

  • Primary lockup: 120px wide on screen, 1 inch wide in print. Below that, switch to the monogram.
  • Monogram: 24px on screen, 0.25 inch in print.

Do's

  • Use the largest file size that exceeds the surface dimensions. Never upscale.
  • Place the primary lockup on Paper, Ink, or a desaturated photograph with adequate contrast.
  • Keep the wordmark horizontal. The lockup is centered, three lines, equal width.

Don'ts

  • Do not stretch, skew, or rotate the lockup.
  • Do not recolor outside the approved files. The four primary lockups (color, mono-black, reverse-cream, reverse-walnut) are the only sanctioned color treatments.
  • Do not add drop shadows, outer glows, bevels, gradients, or wood-grain fills.
  • Do not crop the lockup or remove any of the three lines.
  • Do not place the lockup on a busy or high-contrast photograph without a tinted overlay.
  • Do not pair the wordmark with the old gold-on-navy badge under any circumstance.
  • Do not use "Premier Cabinets" without "Innovations" in any branded surface. The plural is locked.
  • Do not animate the wordmark beyond a static fade-in.

Semantic usage

  • Ink on Paper is the default text pairing. 95% of body copy.
  • Paper on Ink is the inverse, used for hero sections, footers, and any dark editorial surface.
  • Walnut is an accent only. Reserve it for link hovers, focus rings, active-state indicators, and one editorial pull-quote per page maximum. Walnut is never used for body copy.
  • Stone carries hairlines (1px), divider lines, secondary meta labels (PROJECT, LOCATION, YEAR), and disabled UI states.
  • Mist softens section transitions and provides a faint card surface on Paper backgrounds.
  • Shadow is the deeper companion to Ink. Use it when Ink is already in play and a second dark surface is needed.

Pairing rules

  • Two colors per surface is the ceiling. Three only if Walnut appears as a single accent point.
  • Walnut never touches Ink without a Paper or Mist buffer. Walnut on Ink reads muddy. The dedicated primary-reverse-walnut lockup is the only exception, and it is used sparingly.
  • Never pair Stone and Mist on the same surface. They fight.
  • The brand has no red, no green, no blue. No success/error palette. Status messaging uses Ink with a Walnut underline.

Pairing rules

  • Cormorant is reserved for Display, H1, and the wordmark in the logo. Never use Cormorant for body copy.
  • Inter handles everything else: H2 and below, all UI, meta labels, captions, and body.
  • The "gold-word" accent (the gold-colored word inside a headline) inherits Cormorant when inside Display/H1 and Inter when inside H2+.

Weight rules

  • Light (300) is reserved for Display and H1 only. Large editorial headlines. Never used below 32px (Light below 32px reads frail).
  • Regular (400) carries body copy, H2, captions, and most surfaces.
  • Medium (500) is for section labels, meta labels, H3, navigation links, and buttons. Anything that needs to read as a deliberate label.

Letter-spacing rules

  • All-caps text (primary mark, section labels, meta labels, button text) always carries 0.18em tracking. Never less, never more.
  • Sentence-case body text carries default (0) tracking.
  • Display and H1 may tighten to -0.01em / -0.005em to compensate for Light weight at large sizes.

Line-height rules

  • Display and H1: 1.05 to 1.1 for editorial compression.
  • H2 and H3: 1.15 to 1.25.
  • Body and body-large: 1.6 for readable paragraphs.
  • Captions and meta labels: 1.2 to 1.5.

Tabular numerals

Project meta (year, square footage, project number) uses tabular numerals. CSS: font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums. This keeps columns of numbers aligned across project case-study tables.

6. Voice & Tone

Voice principles

  1. Quiet authority. Forty-one years does not need to shout. State the fact. Move on.
  2. Specific over superlative. "Plain-sawn white oak" beats "premium hardwood." "Blum soft-close" beats "quality hardware." Adjectives are an admission that the noun is weak.
  3. Photography does the heavy lifting. Copy supports the image. The image supports the project. Both support the customer's decision.
  4. Restraint. Short sentences. White space. One idea per paragraph. The reader fills in the rest.
  5. No claims we cannot stand on. If a number cannot be verified, do not publish it.

Sample headlines

  • Custom cabinetry and millwork. Since 1985.
  • Built in Citrus Heights. Installed across Northern California.
  • A 41-year shop, building for the way people live in 2026.
  • The cabinets stay. Everything else changes.

Sample body copy

Felix has been building cabinets in Citrus Heights since 1985. The shop runs on plain-sawn white oak, mortise-and-tenon joinery, and finishes that hold up to a working kitchen. Each project starts with a measure, a drawing, and a conversation about how the room is used.
A paneled room is not millwork applied to a wall. It is a wall built from millwork. The framing, the joinery, the moldings, and the panels are designed together and installed as one room.

Forbidden phrases

Never use the following, in any surface:

  • Premier (as a generic adjective. The brand name is the only sanctioned use.)
  • Leading
  • World-class
  • Passionate about
  • Quality you can trust
  • One-stop shop
  • Cutting-edge
  • State-of-the-art
  • Bring your dreams to life
  • We pride ourselves on
  • Quality that sets us apart (this was the old tagline. It is dead.)
  • Our priority is the customer's satisfaction (also dead)
  • 24/7 (we are not 24/7 and would not claim it)
  • Seamless / seamlessly
  • Game changer
  • Leverage (as a verb)

Punctuation rules

  • No em dashes in body copy. Use a period or a comma.
  • No exclamation points. Anywhere.
  • No emoji in customer-facing copy.
  • Oxford comma is used.
  • Numbers under 10 are spelled out. 10 and above are numerals. Years and addresses are always numerals.

7. Photography Direction

The reference is Somerville (somervilles.co.uk). Editorial joinery photography. Architectural framing. Natural light. Silence.

Style

  • Full-bleed by default. Hero images and project covers run edge to edge with no border.
  • Architectural framing. Wide rooms shot at standing eye level. Symmetry where it exists. Vertical lines kept vertical (no leaning walls from wide-angle distortion).
  • Natural light, lifted shadows. Slight desaturation. Shadow detail preserved. No HDR glow. No blown highlights.
  • Selective gallery, not exhaustive. Twelve strong images beat thirty mixed ones.

What to shoot

  • Wide hero of the finished room.
  • Mid-shot of the cabinet run or millwork wall as a single composition.
  • Detail shots: hardware, joinery, edge profiles, drawer interiors.
  • Process: Felix's hands, a jig, raw wood, sawdust. One per project, no more.
  • Materials laid flat: wood samples, hardware specs, finish swatches.

What to avoid

  • People posing in the room.
  • Real-estate-style wide-angle distortion.
  • Stylist props (fake fruit bowls, generic cookbooks, lemons).
  • Pets, kids, drinkware staged on counters.
  • Smartphone shots. All site photography is DSLR or mirrorless full-frame minimum.
  • Visible cords, plugs, switches, smoke detectors. If unavoidable, retouch them out.

Caption treatment

Captions sit below the image in a single meta line. All-caps Medium 500, 11px, Stone color, 18% tracking.

Format:

PROJECT NAME · CITY, STATE · YEAR · MATERIAL HIGHLIGHT

Example:

TAVASSOLI RESIDENCE · LAFAYETTE, CA · 2024 · PLAIN-SAWN WHITE OAK, MARBLE

Project case studies use a longer caption block: location, completion year, scope, primary materials, hardware brand. All in tabular numerals where numbers appear.

10. Brand Don'ts

The ten things to never do, ever.

  1. Never use the old gold-on-navy badge. That logo is dead. Do not retrieve it from any archive. Do not reference it. Do not put it on a "throwback" anywhere.
  2. Never use "Premier" without "Cabinets Innovations" in the lockup. The plural full name is the only sanctioned brand mark.
  3. Never use "Premiere" with an E. Auto-correct will try. Override it every time.
  4. Never use script, serif, slab, or display fonts in any branded surface. The system is Inter only. No exceptions for "fun" applications.
  5. Never add wood-grain textures, wood photographs as background fills, or sawdust overlays to typography or the lockup. The brand references wood through photography, not through pattern.
  6. Never use the old tagline "Quality That Sets Us Apart" or "Our priority is the customer's satisfaction." Both are retired.
  7. Never use exclamation points, em dashes in body copy, or emoji in customer-facing copy.
  8. Never publish a number or claim that cannot be substantiated. No invented years, no invented project counts, no "thousands of happy customers" copy.
  9. Never recolor the logo outside the four approved lockups. No teal, no gold, no red, no gradient versions.
  10. Never stretch, skew, rotate, crop, or animate the wordmark. It is a fixed three-line lockup. Treat it like a printed object, not a flexible asset.
